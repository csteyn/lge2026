**L039. The ward-winner gap (O7): diagnosis, a correction for attenuated slopes, and a pre-registered experiment.** In the blind 2021 backtest the model picked 85.5% of ward winners correctly, against 90.9% for simply using the 2019 national vote, while beating both simple rules on seats.

*Diagnosis from existing evidence.* It is not simply the DA's overshooting premium: variants with all premiums set to zero were no better on wards (about 84.5%). The prime suspect is the attenuation logged in L003 and never corrected. The fitted slopes `b` are biased towards zero by sampling noise in the 2019 shares, which compresses spatial contrasts (which neighbourhoods lean to which party) towards the council average, and ward contests turn on exactly those contrasts. The national-vote rule uses the pattern unsquashed (effectively b = 1), which fits it winning on wards while losing on seats; and the zero-premium variants still used the squashed slopes. Secondary suspects: the ward/PR ticket-splitting ratio carried from the previous local election, and individual premiums that overshoot.

*Correction.* A share p among n votes has log-scale sampling variance of about (1 - p)/(np). Each party's reliability (lambda, the fraction of its observed spatial variance that is real) gives the classical errors-in-variables correction b / lambda, with the intercept re-set to keep the weighted means. On demo data with known truth, the mean slope error halves (0.057 -> 0.024), though DELTA FRONT recovers only from 0.77 to 0.81 of a true 0.90: the correction handles sampling noise, not every source of attenuation. With default settings the model is unchanged, as tested.

*Pre-registered experiment, fixed before any result.* 12 variants on the 2021 backtest: slopes {fitted, corrected, fixed at 1} x ticket-splitting ratio {on, off} x premiums {as fitted, halved}, at the spreads and newcomer setting in config.yml. **Rule:** choose the lowest mean ward Brier score (a proper score using the probabilities, not just the most likely winner) among variants whose mean seat CRPS is at most 0.005 worse than the current model's and whose established-party 90% coverage is in [0.80, 0.97]. Adopt it only if its ward Brier score beats the current model's. If no variant qualifies, including the current one, nothing changes. Ward accuracy, log score, seat error and council control are reported, not optimised. A per-ward table records, for every 2021 ward, the actual winner, the model's pick and the simple rules' picks, so the result can be explained whichever way it goes. **L036. Error found while testing L035: the backtest scores contained avoidable randomness, and one comparison could depend on how a function call was written.** In the demo, "with newcomers" and "without newcomers" gave different seat CRPS (0.5108 against 0.5166) with zero newcomers. Cause: R evaluates function arguments lazily, so in `score_chain(simulate_chain(...))` the scorer set its seed *before* the simulation ran, and the simulation reseeds the generator itself. The score depended on random draws in two places: CRPS was estimated from a random subsample of 600 draws, and PIT coverage used one random number per case. Scoring noise was about 1%. The pre-registered grid (L027-L028) was chosen on CRPS differences of 0.001-0.004 among its top settings, which is within this noise. The surface had been flagged as flat, but not for this reason. Fixes: CRPS for whole-number seats is now computed exactly, as a sum over integers (identical to the old estimate on all draws, and deterministic). Coverage is the exact expected value of the randomised PIT (a closed form for discrete outcomes). The scorer evaluates the simulation before seeding. Newcomers' own random variation comes from a separate stream, so established parties see identical random numbers with and without them, making the newcomer test a paired comparison. The grid settings were already paired (fixed per-council seeds scale the same random numbers), so their ranking noise came only from scoring. **Committed in advance:** the next run rescores the 24 grid settings exactly. If the unchanged L027 rule (lowest CRPS among settings with 90% coverage in [0.80, 0.97]) then picks a different setting, config.yml is changed to it and logged, before the newcomer result is read.

**L035. Newcomer module built; pre-registered test written before any result.** *The problem.* In the blind 2021 backtest, parties the model could not see won 46 Western Cape seats. The 2026 candidate lists name every newcomer: 125 party-council cases, 56 parties, at least one in each of the 25 councils. That is over a quarter of the 480 party-council ballot entries. 108 of the 125 cases are parties with no Western Cape history at all. Most (90) stand in every ward, so ward coverage separates them less than expected; breadth separates them more (39 parties stand in one council, 4 in 11 or more: Afrika Mayibuye Movement 23, Cape Independence Party 22, SACP 21, Organic Humanity Movement 11). The **South African Communist Party**, standing on its own for the first time after contesting historically in alliance with the ANC, is unlike any past Western Cape newcomer; the class prior may describe it poorly (A17).

*The module.* Past newcomers are parties that stood in a council in 2016 or 2021 with no votes there in the previous local or latest national election (matched by conservative name key, so renamed parties are not counted). They are grouped by breadth (1 / 2-10 / 11+ councils) x coverage (all wards / partial), falling back to breadth alone, then to all newcomers, below 8 examples. Each simulated election draws each 2026 newcomer's council share from its class's historical distribution. A party's draws are correlated across councils, with the correlation estimated from past newcomers standing in several councils (default 0.5 with fewer than 10 pairs). The share is inserted after the established parties' shocks, varied across wards and voting districts, and taken proportionally from everyone else (A16). A party-specific prior can replace the class prior only through a sourced row in `data-raw/manual/entrant_priors.csv`.

*An error caught before any use.* The first version inserted newcomers before the shocks. The established parties' province and council shocks are symmetric on the log scale, but their sum is not, so newcomers were diluted by about 14% (median simulated share 6.8% against 7.9% drawn). Inserting after the shocks reproduces each draw exactly (to 10^-17). With no newcomers, the simulation is identical to the previous code in seats, ward winners and shares.

*Pre-registered test.* The 2021 backtest is run with and without the module at the chosen settings (fitted premiums; spreads 0.55 and 0.45, not re-tuned). The newcomer prior comes from **2016's newcomers only**, because using 2021's would be circular; this needs the 2011 local results (IEC id 197). **Rule:** adopt the module if seat CRPS is lower with it AND PIT coverage at 90% (modelled parties) lies in [0.80, 0.97]. If adopted, the 2026 forecast uses a prior pooled from 2016 and 2021 newcomers. The module is off (`model.entrants: false`) until then.

**L023. The Patriotic Alliance's local premium, estimated from by-elections (O5 step 2).** For each voting district of a Western Cape by-election ward in which the PA stood, its vote relative to the parties with a fitted translation was compared with the prediction from that district's 2024 vote; residuals were vote-weighted within wards and averaged across wards. From 14 wards since the 2024 national election, the premium is +0.20 ± 0.19 (log-ratio scale): the PA does somewhat better in local elections than its 2024 national vote implies. Combined by precision with the prior, it is +0.12 ± 0.17, drawn afresh in every simulation. A placebo, the same estimate for fitted parties whose premium is already in the model, came out consistent with zero (ANC −0.05 ± 0.19, DA −0.23 ± 0.23, EFF +0.28 ± 0.40), so no systematic-error term was needed. The window was chosen on that placebo, not on the PA result. Including pre-2024 by-elections, the placebo parties drift (systematic error 0.35), because those by-elections predate the 2024 baseline they are compared with, and the PA estimate falls to −0.18. That sensitivity is recorded here and in A14. **Correction to L022:** the step 1 prior centres on the fitted parties' average premium, −0.32. On its own it would have lowered the PA's expected local share by about a quarter; the evidence points the other way. It was never used in a live run. A prior built from typical parties describes an atypical, locally organised party poorly, which is why step 2 was needed. The National Coloured Congress, the People's Movement for Change and the ATM have fewer than 3 by-election wards in the modelled councils and keep the prior. By-election party names are abbreviations; they are mapped to results names through `byelection_abbreviations.csv`, which lists only mappings we are sure of.

**L022. Parties with no fitted translation no longer get a certain one (step 1 of 2 on O5).** The Patriotic Alliance was not on the 2019 Western Cape provincial ballot (confirmed: no PA rows in the 2019 file), so the 2019 -> 2021 fit has nothing to learn its local premium `a` from. Until now such parties got `a = 0` with no uncertainty, which is an assumption that local and national support are identical, with false confidence. The PA won 28 seats in 21 Western Cape councils in 2021, and in 14 of them no party had a majority; its 2021 share was 22% in Beaufort West, 14% in Prince Albert and Laingsburg, and 12.5% in Matzikama. Now `a` is a distribution. The prior is the mean and spread of `a` among the fitted parties, drawn afresh in each simulation and shared across councils within a draw (a party's local premium is a property of the party). This widens intervals where such parties are large; it does not correct any bias. Step 2 will estimate the premium from the PA's by-election results since 2021 and combine that with this prior by precision. That step will be built against the real by-election data, not a guessed layout (A13).

**L021. Disputes resolved, and a modelling error found through them: votes of parties no longer standing were being given to small parties.** On re-checking, local knowledge agrees with the certified list: none of the five flagged parties is standing in 2026 under any name, so the two disputed entries (L020) are now confirmed absences and C15 has no open items. Working out what that means for the forecast exposed an error. When the candidate data showed a party was not standing, the code dropped it from the party table, so its past votes fell through to the catch-all OTHER, which is later split among the small parties when seats are allocated. GOOD's 8% in Beaufort West, or the Karoo Gemeenskap Party's 11% in Prince Albert, was being handed to minor parties. A test reproduced it: a non-standing party with 10% tripled OTHER from 5% to 15%. Fix: non-standing parties are kept and marked absent, and their votes are removed and the standing parties' shares renormalised within each voting district, both in the 2026 baseline and in the 2019 -> 2021 transfer fit and ward/PR ratios. That redistribution is itself an assumption, now A12.

**L019. Error, found by check C14: we applied the excessive-seat rule on equality; the IEC does not.** Validated against the IEC's published 2021 calculations for all 25 Western Cape councils, 19 matched and 6 failed. In five (Matzikama, Cederberg, Kannaland, Bitou, Prince Albert) the seats were identical, but we flagged a party as "excessive" because its ward wins equalled its entitlement, and the IEC did not. In Beaufort West the seats differed: the ANC won 4 wards with an entitlement of exactly 4; treating that as excessive fixed the ANC at 4, recalculated the other 9 seats without it, and moved one seat from the DA to GOOD (ours DA 3, GOOD 2; IEC DA 4, GOOD 1). In a 13-seat council one seat can decide control. With the trigger changed to strictly greater, all 25 councils are reproduced exactly, including the three genuine excessive-seat cases (Mossel Bay, Oudtshoorn, Laingsburg), whose parties match the IEC's own markings. We had adopted "equal to or greater" from the Johannesburg model's documentation without testing it on a council where equality occurs; its own Johannesburg validation would not have exposed the difference. This also closes assumption A03, since a party with no wards can never strictly exceed its entitlement. Also fixed: the workbook reader took a party name as the council code in three councils; it now reads the "Municipality:" row only. All 25 councils are now regression tests, and the test that had asserted the old rule was reversed.

**L018. Candidate lists: the real layout, and three problems it exposed.** The IEC's Western Cape list (16 September, 271 pages) is one table with no section headings; the "Ward \ List Order" column holds either a PR list position or an 8-digit ward ID, and that is what separates ward candidates from PR lists. Our parser allowed at most 4 digits there, so every ward row failed (O4 resolved). Rewritten around this, it parses 4 502 PR rows (IEC: 4 502) and 9 846 ward rows (IEC: 9 847) covering all 416 wards in 30 councils. The missing ward candidate is a Drakenstein row printed with a malformed ID (`102010****0 *`); it now parses, and its impossible date yields no birth year rather than a false one (1910). Our quarantine count had also missed that row, because it only counted rows with a well-formed ID; it now counts every table row that fails to parse. Most seriously, the PDF prints some party names bilingually (`VRYHEIDSFRONT PLUS | FREEDOM FRONT PLUS`), unlike the results files; matched naively, the FF+ would have been treated as absent from every 2026 ballot. Names are now harmonised against the results files. New check C15 lists every party with 2%+ in 2021 or 2024 that is not on a council's 2026 PR list, for a human to confirm; C16 compares parsed candidate counts with the IEC's published totals.

**L015. Error, found on the first live simulation: "missing value where TRUE/FALSE needed" inside a council simulation.** The memory fix (L014) held: the transfer fit, the baseline and 25 simulation branches ran on real data for the first time. One branch then failed deep in the seat arithmetic. The exact cause is not confirmed, but the code had two routes by which a single missing value could turn a whole council's votes into `NA`. First, `weighted.mean(na.rm = TRUE)` drops missing values but not missing weights, so one voting district with a blank registration count made its ward and municipality averages `NA`. Second, a ward or VD spread that the transfer fit could not estimate would have been passed on as `NA`. Fixes: registration gaps are imputed from ward, then municipality, medians and counted (check C12); turnout imputation is counted (C13); averages ignore incomplete pairs; any share still missing is filled explicitly. The simulation now checks its inputs first and stops with the named cause, and the seat allocator refuses non-finite votes by party name. The lesson: validate at the boundary between data and arithmetic, where the cause is still visible.

**L014. **L006. **L005. Decision: OTHER never wins seats as a bloc.** Its votes are split into its member parties in fixed proportions before seats are allocated, and a ward "won by OTHER" goes to its largest member (A04). Consequence: seat counts for very small parties are rough.

**L004. Error, fixed before publication: baseline shares in imputed VDs summed to 0.97, not 1.** Cause: new VDs averaged *log* shares across their ward, which is a geometric mean, and by Jensen's inequality the result sums to less than one. The simulation renormalises, so no output was affected, but the baseline file was wrong. Fix: renormalise every VD in `build_baseline()`; a test now asserts sums of exactly one.

**L003. Fix: a party must have real votes in both elections in a municipality to inform the fit. Also found, **not fixed**: slopes are attenuated by noise in the predictor (0.78 fitted against 0.90 true on synthetic data). Logged as a known bias in METHODOLOGY; check D01 tracks it in demo runs.
